# Reference Brief

## 1. Executive summary

The page introduces Johnny.Decimal, a system to organise information with numbered folders. It uses a filing-cabinet analogy: a computer is a room, an area is a cabinet, a category is a drawer, and an ID is a folder. It gives four benefits of the ID: structure, easy communication, stable position, and limits on choice. The page shows no author and no date.

## 2. Central premise and author intent

Premise: files kept without a numbered structure are hard to find, and a fixed numeric structure fixes this. The page is the official introduction on the system's own site. Its intent is to teach and to promote the system (inferred from the page type and publisher).

## 3. Key takeaways

- The system has three levels: area, category, ID. Area folders are limited to 10.
- Each cabinet (area) holds up to 10 drawers (categories). A category holds no more than 100 IDs.
- The ID gives the exact place of an item and can be said aloud.
- Numbers keep folders in place. Alphabetical names make folders move when a new one is added.
- The limits narrow the search at each level.

## 4. Facts and figures

- Areas: 10 filing cabinets in total.
- Categories: space for 10 drawers in each cabinet.
- IDs: no more than 100 in a category folder.
- Example: three cabinets used (`life admin`, `home business`, `tennis club`). Five drawers in `life admin` (`me`, `house`, `money`, `online`, `travel`).
- Example: IDs start at `.11`.

## 5. Methods and frameworks

- Three-step build (the source's own steps): (1) buy 10 filing cabinets; (2) assign drawers; (3) file items in numbered folders.
- Analogy: computer = room, area = filing cabinet, category = drawer, ID = manila folder.

## 6. Concepts and definitions

- Area: a top-level folder (a filing cabinet in the analogy).
- Category: a folder in an area (a drawer).
- ID: the folder where files are saved (a manila folder). The numbers before the decimal point are the category.
- The page promises a later explanation of why the first drawer is not `01` or `10`. This page does not give it.
