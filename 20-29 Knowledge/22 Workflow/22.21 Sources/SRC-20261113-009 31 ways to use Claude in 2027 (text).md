# 31 Life-Changing Ways to Use Claude in 2027

**Channel:** Nick Saraev
**Length:** ~14 min
**Type:** Educational / listicle
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KDyPHt4lW60

## TL;DR
Most people use Claude like a fancy email writer, and Saraev's point is that it can do way more right now: hack your own gadgets, replace paid apps, run research, build learning tools. It's a highlight reel of real people's projects, mostly from social media, not a how-to.

## Key Takeaways
- **You can replace software you pay for.** Pieter Levels cut his bills to about six by having Claude build replacements; someone else rebuilt a baby-tracking app after it went paid.
- **Claude can turn piles of content into something dense.** One person fed it 40 hours of podcasts and got a compact playbook.
- **Visual planning works today.** Give it room dimensions and photos and it builds a 3D walkthrough to test whether furniture fits.
- **Old hardware gets a second life.** Old iPads, Kindles, and phones become dashboards and smart-home panels.
- **Many agents beat one.** Big wins (cancer research, planet hunting, a new enzyme) came from running lots of agents over huge data.
- **Better models negotiate better.** In Anthropic's agent marketplace test, stronger models got better prices, and weaker ones didn't notice.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Intro (0:00–0:31)
Paying for Claude and only writing emails is a waste; AI can do far more than people realize.

### Hacking and Home Projects (0:31–2:08)
- A TV rebuilt with no ads and a calendar/weather screensaver.
- A custom dictation mic: Claude picked parts, wrote firmware, and designed a 3D-printed case.
- A slick air-conditioner controller built from notes in a couple of hours.
- Jailbroken Kindles turned into home dashboards.

### System Optimization (2:08–3:29)
- A slow router tuned for an 8x speed gain.
- A receipt printer that prints a morning email "newspaper."
- A DJI drone turned into an offline home security patrol linked to an alarm.
- A 2010-era iPad repurposed as a smart-home wall panel.

### Lifestyle and Subscriptions (3:29–4:31)
- A $60 used BlackBerry running Claude Code.
- Pieter Levels replacing most of his subscriptions with Claude-built apps.

### Agent Negotiations (4:31–5:03)
- Anthropic's experiment: 69 employees' agents closed about 200 deals among themselves; stronger models got better prices.
- Someone automated Hinge swiping with preference files and got about 25 matches a day.

### Security (5:03–6:32)
- A temperature monitor that uncovered a hidden crypto miner.
- A Civic head unit found to have an always-on mic and a vendor back door.
- A hotel/Airbnb sweep for cameras, trackers, and spyware.

### Biomedical Research (6:32–7:34)
- Miriam, a software engineer with stage 4 breast cancer, ran about 30 agents to research her case; Dana-Farber reviewed and improved the biopsy plan, and Anthropic gave her $30K in credits.
- A solo-operator biotech, reportedly acquired by Anthropic.

### Scientific Discovery (7:34–8:31)
- About 1,500 Claude-written scripts searched seven years of NASA data and found a planet candidate.
- Anthropic's ~1,000 agents found a new CRISPR-like enzyme.

### Education and Construction (8:31–10:06)
- A French dub of a kids' game using local voice models.
- A 9-year-old's website built by talking to Claude Code.
- 3D room planners for a bed and for picture-frame placement.

### Data and Genealogy (10:06–10:57)
- Walkthrough houses from floor plans for about $5 in tokens.
- A five-generation family tree from one birth record.
- The baby-app rebuild.

### Skills and Learning (10:57–11:48)
- A drone flight simulator built as a Claude skill.
- The podcast-to-playbook trick.

### Games (11:48–12:52)
- Decompiling Modern Warfare 2, reviving 3DS games, restoring The Witcher 3's old look.
- Porting games without emulators.

### Creative Strategy (12:52–13:32)
- Mapping ~150,000 Steam games to find concept gaps nobody has built.

### Final Thoughts (13:32–end)
- Overnight photography, market research, fitness and health tracking.
- Ends with a pitch for his paid Maker School.

## Critical Notes
- **Showcase, not tutorial.** No steps here; the prompts are behind his description link.
- **Mostly unverified social posts.** Treat the 8x Wi-Fi and 60% dating-match numbers as anecdotes. The Anthropic marketplace and enzyme claims are worth fact-checking at the source.
- **Legal and ToS gray zones.** Jailbreaking devices, automating Hinge, and decompiling commercial games can break terms of service or copyright. He mostly skips that.
- **Model talk is speculative.** The bit about Anthropic running "Fable 5.5 or Mythos 6" is him guessing, not a fact.
- **Ends in a sales pitch** for Maker School.
- **Medical caveat.** The cancer story is about research support reviewed by real doctors, not Claude replacing them.
