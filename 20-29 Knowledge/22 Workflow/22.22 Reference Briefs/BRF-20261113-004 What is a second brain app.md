## 1. Executive summary

This is a vendor FAQ article from Nemos, a company that makes an iPhone note app. It defines a "second brain app" as a personal knowledge system that captures, organizes, and resurfaces what you encounter, and it says that four functions define such an app in 2026. It gives a short history of the idea (Memex, Zettelkasten, commonplace books, Forte's book), compares tools, and lists pitfalls. Its main verdict is that consistent use for more than 60 days matters more than the choice of tool. Several numbers come from the publisher's own survey, and the article does not describe that survey.

## 2. Central premise and author intent

Premise: a second brain app removes the need to remember what you capture, and the 2026 tools add AI retrieval to an old idea. Intent: to answer a common search question and to place the publisher's product (Nemos: "iPhone-first, on-device AI", free tier) among its competitors. The article is marketing content. It includes a promotional call-out and an App Store button. The author is shown as Taha Baalla, "Updated May 14, 2026".

## 3. Key takeaways

- The source defines a second brain app as a personal knowledge system that captures, organizes, and resurfaces information (C1).
- The source says that a 2026 second brain app does four things: it captures fast, organizes automatically, retrieves instantly, and resurfaces proactively (C7).
- The source says the idea is older than the term. It names the Memex (1945), Luhmann's Zettelkasten, and commonplace books (C4, C5).
- The source says you probably do not need one if you already have a working system, save fewer than about 50 items each month, or prefer pen and paper (C9).
- The source says that consistency for more than 60 days predicts success better than the choice of tool (C19), and that most users feel the benefit only in month 4 or 5 (C18).
- The source lists six pitfalls: tool churn, capture without distillation, no review, privacy and lock-in, mixing personal and professional content, and dependence on AI (C20, C21).
- The time-saving figure (6.4 hours each month) comes from the publisher's own survey, and the article does not describe the method (C17).

## 4. Facts and figures

All figures are as the source states them. I did not verify any of them.

- Tiago Forte's book on the second brain was published in 2022 (C3). The source says it sold more than 100,000 copies in its first year (C16). The first BASB cohort was in 2017.
- Vannevar Bush's 1945 Atlantic Monthly essay "As We May Think" proposed the Memex (C4).
- Luhmann's Zettelkasten: "90,000 index cards producing 70+ books" (C5). Dates in the source: "in the 1960s" (top section) and "1951-1997" (The deeper story).
- If capture takes more than 3 seconds, the user will not do it. Retrieval should take 5 seconds (C7, C8).
- AI-native tools (Mem, Reflect, Tana, Capacities) cost $10–30 per month (C13).
- Apple Notes is the default for about 40% of surveyed iPhone users (C14). The source does not identify the survey.
- Mem pivoted in 2023 (C15).
- The average surveyed user saved 6.4 hours each month (2026 survey by the publisher) (C17).
- Threshold for need: under 50 items each month (C9). Threshold for worth: 50 or more articles each month (§ The 2026 verdict).
- Forum threads on r/PKMS and r/Notion get 50 to 200 comments of conflicting advice (§ Why this question gets asked so often).

- Memex: Vannevar Bush's 1945 proposal, named as an ancestor (C4).
- Zettelkasten: Niklas Luhmann's index-card system, named as an ancestor (C4, C5).
- Commonplace book: a historical precedent. The source names Marcus Aurelius and Francis Bacon in "The deeper story".
- Target users (§ Who needs one?): students and academics, founders and consultants, creators, and knowledge workers.
- Terms in the source that it does not define: "semantic search", "on-device AI", "local-first", "distill".

## 10. Limitations and caveats

- Conflict of interest: the publisher sells a second brain app and rates its competitors in the same article. The tool ratings (C2, C10 to C13) and the on-device AI claim are not neutral.
- The survey figures (C14, C17, C18) come from surveys that the article does not describe: no sample, no method, no questions. The 6.4-hour figure is self-reported.
- The source gives no citations for its historical or market figures (C5, C16).
- Internal inconsistency: the source dates the Zettelkasten "in the 1960s" in one section and "1951-1997" in another.
- The source says the term comes from the 2022 book (C3), but it also places Forte's first BASB cohort in 2017. The origin date of the term is therefore not clear in the source.
- Tool features and prices change fast. Prices are as of August 2026, based on a footer note that is outside the scope of this Brief.
- I did not verify any fact against another source, because the Reporter rules do not allow other sources.
- The `url` host (nemos-landing.pages.dev) differs from the canonical URL that the page states (nemosapp.com/faq/what-is-a-second-brain-app/). The content appears to be the same page.
