## 1. Executive summary

A YouTube video by Nick Saraev (published 2026-11-02, about 14 min) lists about 31 uses of Claude and Claude Code. The examples are home hardware hacks, device repurposing, subscription replacement, security checks, research with many agents, learning tools, game modding, and idea mapping. Almost all examples are second-hand stories of unnamed people. The transcript gives no source, link, or data for them. The author says that "AI can do a lot more than most people think". The video ends with a pitch for his paid course, Maker School.

## 2. Central premise and author intent

Premise: most people use Claude for small tasks such as emails, but current models can do much more ("capabilities overhang", 0:24). Intent: to show a "hit list" of uses that viewers probably do not know (0:08), to send viewers to prompts behind a description link (0:16), and to promote Maker School (14:12). The format is a showcase. It is not a tutorial. The transcript gives no steps or prompts.

## 3. Key takeaways

- The author's view: AI can do much more than most people think (C1).
- Hardware and devices: the source reports people who removed TV ads, built a dictation microphone, built an air-conditioner interface, jailbroke Kindles, tuned a router, and repurposed a receipt printer, a drone, an old iPad, and a BlackBerry with Claude (C2 to C10).
- Subscriptions: the source reports one person who replaced most software subscriptions with apps built by Claude, and one person who rebuilt a baby-tracking app (C11, C33).
- Agent negotiation: the source reports an Anthropic test in which agents of 69 employees closed almost 200 deals. In that test, stronger models got cheaper prices (C12, C13). The author generalizes from this test (C14).
- Security: the source reports a hidden crypto miner, an always-listening car head unit with a vendor back door, and a room sweep for cameras and trackers (C16 to C18).
- Research with many agents: the source reports a cancer patient who used about 30 agents, a solo biotech that Anthropic bought, a planet candidate in NASA data, and an Anthropic enzyme discovery (C19 to C26).
- Learning, planning, and games: the source reports 3D room planners, genealogy, a drone simulator skill, a podcast-to-playbook digest, game decompiling, and a map of gaps between Steam games (C27 to C41).

- A general pattern repeats in the examples: give Claude or Claude Code access to a device or data (store listing, photo, notes, birth record, transcripts), ask in plain language, and let it build, diagnose, or analyze (source description; my summary of the pattern).
- Many agents on large data: the source describes many parallel agents or scripts on a large data set (NASA data, a DNA database, cancer research) (6:49, 7:41, 8:06).
- Gap mapping: plot existing items (Steam games) on a dimensional graph, then ask Claude to find the empty spaces between them (12:52–13:09).

## 10. Limitations and caveats

- Almost all examples are second-hand. They are unnamed or loosely named people, with no link, date, or data in the transcript. Each example is one case. Together they do not show how often these results occur.
- The source names no study or post for the Anthropic negotiation test (C12, C13), the enzyme discovery (C26), the Miriam case (C19, C20), or the Cody acquisition (C23). These claims need a check at their primary sources.
- Several figures are approximate or uncertain to the speaker: 5 or 10 Mbps, "2010 or 2012", "something like 30", "something like 1500", "probably like 100x".
- The transcript has internal inconsistencies:
  - "Planet candidate" (7:58) and "two additional planets" (7:58).
  - "Revoice them using another service" (8:54) and "free local voice models" (9:03).
- The speaker generalizes from single cases (C14, C22, C31, C39, C41, C42).
- The speaker's guess about Anthropic models ("could probably run the Fable 5.5 or Mythos 6", 8:37) is a guess.
- Legal, ethical, and terms-of-service issues are mostly not discussed. Examples are device jailbreaks, Hinge automation, and game decompiling. The speaker declines to discuss the ethics of the negotiation and Hinge uses (4:39).
- Medical: the source says Miriam has not cured her cancer (6:49). A lab reviewed the biopsy plan (6:57). The personalized-vaccine outcome is speculation (7:04).
- The tone is promotional, and the video ends with a sales pitch (14:12).
- The transcript appears machine-generated. It has many errors: "clawed", "claw", or "cloud" for Claude; "Crisper"; "Dana Farbar"; "Peter Levelvel"; "Digi drone"; "Cuber sonnet".
